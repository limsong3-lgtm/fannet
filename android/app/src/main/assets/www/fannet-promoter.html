Promoter app, Gamma build

The Gamma build, with expanded sports. This app is for anyone who puts on pro events: a combat promoter (as in beta), a football club running its home match days in the Pacific Pro League, and a tennis tournament director. Each one sends their work to the body that sanctions it: the commission, the league or the federation. Pick who you're viewing as, then tap around in the phone or jump to a step below.

What's new in the Gamma build
  • One app for anyone who puts on pro events. View as switches between Alex Morgan (Pacific Fight Promotions, combat, unchanged from beta), Olivia Grant (club operations, Harbour City FC, Pro Football) and Rachel Moss (tournament director, Melbourne Indoor Open, Pro Tennis). The bottom tabs, their order and icons stay as in beta; only what's inside each tab changes.
  • The league sets the fixtures. A club doesn't create league matches. It runs its home match days (ticketing, kick-off time and venue requests) and sends a squad sheet for every match. The Pacific Pro League appoints the officials and approves squad sheets and fixture changes, the same way a commission sanctions a combat event.
  • Squad sheet rule is a sample: 11 starting players including a goalkeeper, up to 7 substitutes, registered players only.
  • Tennis draws. Draw of 32 with 8 seeds from the entry ranking, 3 main draw wildcards and 4 qualifier places. Seeds 1 and 2 go to the top and bottom lines, 3 and 4 by lot, then 5 to 8; everyone else by lot. The entry list and draw go to the sanctioning federation for approval. Melbourne Indoor Open is approved and into round 2; Docklands Indoor Challenge is a sample draft to try Make the draw and Submit (grant Nora Blake's wildcard in Openings to complete it).
  • Openings work through agents. Club trial invites, loan and transfer enquiries and contract offers, and tournament invites, go to the player's agent when they have one, or to the player directly. Clubs and tournaments never see players' phone, email, registration numbers or date of birth.
  • No documents at sign-up. The new viewers show "ID check passed" in Me and have no ID documents to upload.
  • News adds Pro Football, Pro Basketball and Pro Tennis chips for the new viewers, with their own sport first and in My feed. The beta promoter's news is unchanged.
  • All names are made up: clubs, leagues, tournaments, federations and people.
What changed from the old mocks
  • Passport flag (Lim, 4 Oct): when picking a fighter, a fighter whose country is different from the venue's country and who has no passport on file gets a red "No passport · may not travel" tag. Tapping them warns, with Pick anyway, Ask the manager (or fighter) to add a passport, or Pick someone else. Warn only: the offer can still go out. A local fighter with no passport gets a grey "local only" tag. A Passport check bar counts them and has a "Passport on file only" filter. Openings listings show the tag too. Matchmakers use this same app, so they see it as well.
  • Fannet branding replaces Ringside everywhere.
  • The whole job works on the phone. The old mobile mocks were a cut-down version, with event building and sanction left to the web desk.
  • Offers go out with the bout. When adding a bout, the promoter either picks both fighters and sends both offers with details, or picks one fighter and posts the other corner to Openings with an open offer and an expiry (3, 7, 14 or 21 days). The picked fighter's offer is held until the promoter picks an opponent from the applicants; then both offers go out together. An offer never goes to one fighter without an opponent. Harbour Lights has a sample open slot.
  • Gender and ±2 divisions drive the fighter list, and Fannet Prediction replaces "Instant Prediction".
  • Running purse total on every event: the fight card shows the estimated total, split into accepted, offered and still-estimated, and it updates as you add bouts or send offers.
  • Withdraw an offer by swiping left on a waiting offer in Offers. The corner opens again for a new offer.
  • Sports on a new event come from the promoter's profile (Me, My sports). Each one starts with its home commission as the sanctioning commission.
  • Add bouts any time. "+ Add bout" stays on every event, even after it is submitted or sanctioned. Adding a bout puts the event back to Pending (a sanctioned event loses its label), and the card has to be resubmitted to the commission. Try it on Pacific Fight Night.
  • No sanction with a rejected bout. If the commission rejects any bout, the event shows "Not sanctioned" and offers stay closed. The promoter replaces the fighters or deletes the bout, then resubmits.
  • News is new. Combat sports stories are clipped automatically from news sites. It's its own tab in the bottom bar, filtered to your sports, and keeps loading older stories as you scroll down. Pull down at the top to refresh with new stories. Press and drag the tab row (My feed, All sports, each sport) left or right to see the ones off screen; a fade shows there's more. Stories show as Compact (thumbnail and headline) or Expanded (big image, summary, Read and Share buttons). Share sends a link out to other apps only, not between Fannet users. The gear on the News tab sets My feed: sports, type of story (results, announcements, rankings, rules, interviews, promotion news) and region or country. Each shows the headline, source, time and a short summary, and links out to the full story. The headlines here are made-up samples.
  • Home is about you. Every Fannet app opens with the user's own header: photo, Fannet ID and their track record. For the promoter that's the promotion, licence, sports, events and bouts promoted, plus offers out and cards in review.
  • Search is in every app, as a search bar right under the About Me header on Home. The gear at the top of Home opens Me (profile, roles, settings). It finds people (fighters, promoters, managers, officials), events and commissions. Details opens a full-screen history (a fighter's every bout with result, method, opponent record and event; a promoter's events; an event's fight card; a commission's recent events). Tap any bout in a fighter's history to expand it (one at a time; opening another closes the last): commission, promoter, matchmaker, all officials (referee, judges, timekeeper, doctor, inspector) and each judge's scorecard. For stoppages it shows the time and the cards at the stoppage. Forward shares a link to the result in another app (messages, email, copy link, the phone's share menu). It doesn't send between Fannet users.
  • Chats open into a chat window with the running history and a reply box. Every chat belongs to one event and you can reply until 72 hours after it. After that it's read only for 7 more days, then deleted.
  • Openings switch. On each event (and when creating one), the promoter chooses whether it shows in Openings, where fighters and managers can find it and ask for a slot.
  • Purse currency. The promoter picks the event's local currency when creating it (for example KRW for a Korean card) and can change it until the first offer goes out. Every bout on the card, including bouts with foreign fighters, uses that one currency.
  • Offer purse starts at zero. Switching between Red and Blue in Send offer resets the purse to 0 for a corner with no offer yet, so it never looks like the other fighter's number. The card estimate shows underneath as a guide.
  • W-L-D records use one color standard across FMS and every Fannet app: wins green, losses red, draws blue.
  • Edit any event. "Change" on an event opens Edit event (name, date, time, venue, sports, commissions, currency, Openings). Even a sanctioned event can be changed, but any change other than the purse currency takes it back to Draft, removes the Sanctioned label, and notifies every fighter, manager and commission on the card. The currency locks once offers are sent.
  • Title fights. Turn on "Title fight" in Add bout, or swipe any bout on the fight card left and tap Title fight to add one to an existing matchup. Pick the sanctioning body that owns the belt (WBC, WBA, IBF, WBO, IBO for boxing; WMC, WBC Muaythai, ISKA for Muay), then the title, then Vacant or which corner defends. A title needs two approvals: the local commission and the title owner. The title owner assigns the officials for that bout, and rounds are agreed with them, so the app doesn't force a number. The title lists are common ones for now; sanctioning bodies will register their own later. Pacific Fight Night's main event is a sample.
  • Rule of roles. Promoter, manager and fighter can be held by one account and switched between in Me (a role you don't hold yet shows "+" and starts its sign-up). Officials and fans are stand-alone accounts: they can't be added here, and an official or fan account can't hold any other role.
  • Sports with no bouts. If the event lists a sport (say Pro Muay) but the card has no bouts in it, Submit warns the promoter and offers to send only to the commissions that match the bouts. The empty sport comes off the event. Harbour Lights shows this.
  • Sport names match FMS: Pro Boxing, Pro Muay, Pro KickBoxing, Pro Kun Khmer, MMA.
